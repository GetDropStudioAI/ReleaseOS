## 1. Day in the life of an RTE / enterprise release manager

### 1.1 The SAFe RTE (scaled-agile side)

- Official definition: "The Release Train Engineer (RTE) is a servant leader and ART coach who facilitates ART events and processes, and supports teams in delivering value." Listed duties: coaches the ART through backlog refinement ahead of PI Planning; facilitates key ART events; "Helps optimize flow with metrics and coaching on flow accelerators"; facilitates value stream mapping; "Drives relentless improvement and shares insights with VMO and LACE." — [framework.scaledagile.com/release-train-engineer](https://framework.scaledagile.com/release-train-engineer/)
- Events the RTE runs: PI Planning ("the most important event the RTE role facilitates"), Scrum of Scrums / ART Sync ("like a train-level stand-up"), PO Sync, System Demo, Inspect & Adapt. — [scaledagile.com blog](https://scaledagile.com/blog/excel-at-the-rte-role-with-these-facilitation-tips/), [agilemania.com](https://agilemania.com/release-train-engineer)
- PI Planning outputs the RTE owns and walks weekly: **Team PI Objectives** ("Teams write them in business language... Business Owners assign business value to each one on day two, and the objectives split into committed and uncommitted"), the **Program Board** ("Features, cross-team dependencies, and milestones laid out visually across the Planning Interval's iterations, giving the train a single artifact its ongoing coordination cadence can walk against week by week"), **ROAMed risks** (Resolved/Owned/Accepted/Mitigated), and the **fist-of-five confidence vote**. Boundary: "an RTE who starts arbitrating what belongs in the plan has stepped outside the role." — [agility-at-scale.com/safe/pi-planning](https://agility-at-scale.com/safe/pi-planning/)
- Metric: **ART Predictability Measure** = planned business value (scored at PI Planning) vs. achieved business value (scored at PI end); "The healthy predictability band runs roughly 80 to 100 percent." — same source
- Extended responsibility list from SAFe training providers (secondary): "Understand and be able to visualize and manage upstream/downstream dependencies", "Ensure that the program's overall cost, timelines, and performance fall between pre-set parameters", "Drive cross-team collaboration in the ART and resolve departmental impediments", monitor risks/impediments. — [premieragile.com](https://premieragile.com/release-train-engineer-responsibilities/)
- Facilitation reality *(practitioner opinion, Scaled Agile staff)*: "Book calendars in advance" for 125+ participants, use an "ART Readiness Workbook", prepare "a detailed facilitator's agenda" with scripts and timeboxes; the program board was "built in SAFe Collaborate at PI Planning" then maintained during SoS via "an iteration-by-iteration cross-team dependency board." — [scaledagile.com blog](https://scaledagile.com/blog/excel-at-the-rte-role-with-these-facilitation-tips/)

### 1.3 Recurring rituals (enterprise release manager)

A consolidated ritual table from a role blueprint — [devopsschool.com](https://www.devopsschool.com/blog/release-manager-role-blueprint-responsibilities-skills-kpis-and-career-path/):

| Ritual | Cadence | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Release readiness standup | Per release train | Confirm scope, risks, dependencies, go/no-go criteria |
| Cross-team dependency review | Weekly | Map and resolve sequencing issues |
| CAB meeting | Weekly or per change policy | Formal approvals with risk review |
| Cutover call / war room | During deployment window | Coordinate steps, confirm checkpoints, manage decisions |
| Post-release validation checkpoint | Post-deployment | Smoke tests, monitoring checks, business verification |
| Post-release retrospective / PIR | After major release or incident | Document learnings, assign actions |
| Release calendar maintenance | Ongoing | Resolve scheduling conflicts, align with business events |

**Go/No-Go meeting (Fedora, a mature public example):** facilitator notifies 3 days ahead; representatives from Development, Release Engineering, and QA must be identified at the start; QA verifies "release criteria are met" per the blocker-bug process; release is "GOLD" only when all criteria met, no unaddressed accepted blockers, validation testing complete, and all three groups approve — the process allows "no discretion"; on no-go "the complete release slips by one week"; GOLD is irreversible and is announced by email to three lists. — [fedoraproject.org/wiki/Go_No_Go_Meeting](https://fedoraproject.org/wiki/Go_No_Go_Meeting). The separate **Release Readiness Meeting** happens after go/no-go; each team documents readiness on a wiki page before the meeting; output is per-team status and confirmation they'll be ready on release day. — [fedoraproject.org/wiki/Release_Readiness_Meetings](https://fedoraproject.org/wiki/Release_Readiness_Meetings)

**Go/No-Go as confirmation, not debate** *(practitioner opinion)*: the meeting's question is "are we confident this version will work as expected in the real world, not just in testing?" Root cause of bad go/no-go meetings: "readiness treated as a moment rather than an ongoing process" — information scattered across tools, unclear ownership, risks hidden until the final meeting, "people seeing full picture for first time under time pressure"; when "QA is raising risks, engineering is saying it works on our side, product is worried about timelines" it becomes a negotiation. Fix: "a single shared view of criteria, risks, and status, reviewed regularly". "When it goes right, it feels almost uneventful." — [qualitybridgeconsulting.substack.com](https://qualitybridgeconsulting.substack.com/p/the-gono-go-meeting-should-be-a-confirmation)

**Go/No-Go decision record** *(practitioner opinion, SRE-oriented)*: keep "a compact document" recording time stamps, gate states, evidence links, decision owners, and "any yellow-state exceptions with expiration dates." Separate roles: a Decision Authority who "makes the go/no-go call for the current risk tier" and a Release Commander who "owns pause or abort execution"; "The decision authority must be allowed to say no without pressure from a public deadline." Checklist includes "immutable artifact digest and source revision", "no unreviewed difference from the tested candidate", rollback artifact availability. — [oneuptime.com](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-08-06-launch-day-go-no-go/view)

**Stage gates (10-gate model)** with owner / evidence / fail action: Build Verification (build owner), Smoke (release owner), Sanity (engineer who made the fix), System, Integration/E2E (QA owner), Non-Functional Thresholds (perf/SRE), Acceptance UAT/OAT (business owner/ops – "Release held pending sign-off"), Pre-Production Shakeout, Deployment Verification (deploy engineer – "Automatic rollback triggered"), Canary. "Every gate here is really an entry and exit criterion for the next one." — [getautonoma.com](https://getautonoma.com/blog/release-management-process)

**CAB operating procedure (concrete example):** "CAB meets regularly on Thursdays at 1:00 pm"; RFCs must be submitted before the weekly meeting; IT alerts distributed "7 days prior to the effective date"; permanent members each have a named backup and "In the event that a member (or the designate) does not attend the CAB meeting, they implicitly approve all CAB decisions"; agenda = previous decisions/open actions, "Review and possibly approve new change RFCs", "Forward Schedule of Changes review and agreement", standard-change applications, PIR discussions; requestors answer Yes/N/A to five criteria (DR planning, backup scheduling, testing completion, IT alert distribution, vulnerability assessment); emergency changes: "A Change Request must be entered after change implementation" and go through "PIR process during the CAB meeting following the change". — [Creighton University CAB procedures](https://myservices.creighton.edu/TDClient/32/Portal/KB/Article/1065/Change-Advisory-Board-CAB-Operating-Procedures)

### 1.4 Artifacts they produce

From the blueprint — [devopsschool.com](https://www.devopsschool.com/blog/release-manager-role-blueprint-responsibilities-skills-kpis-and-career-path/): release calendar (enterprise view by product/service/environment); release plan (scope, dependencies, sequencing, cutover runbook); go/no-go checklist with documented acceptance criteria; risk register; RACI; release runbook ("step-by-step with verification and rollback"); communication plan; release notes pack; "Change records with linked evidence (work items, code, test results, approvals)"; "Release completion report (what shipped, validation outcome, known issues)"; release dashboards; retrospective notes with tracked actions; standard templates (release plan, emergency change, cutover agenda).

Technical release manager view: "Maintain a release calendar that aligns with product milestones", "Define release windows and frequency", "Track changes through systems like JIRA, ServiceNow, or Change Request Boards", "Facilitate Go/No-Go meetings when needed", "Prepare customer-facing release notes", manually "Trigger pipeline from a controlled, logged environment" and "Validate release in production (smoke tests or sanity checks)". — [hector-reyesaleman.medium.com](https://hector-reyesaleman.medium.com/what-does-a-release-manager-do-ff468df1a88b)

### 1.5 What they spend time on manually *(practitioner accounts)*

- "It would often take me 6–8 hours to prepare the release plan template and the batch scripts"; hand-edited scripts mapping apps→versions→servers; used a SharePoint deployment request form, "Excel-based release plan templates", and "AutoHotKey automation for filling out change request forms"; ran Scrum of Scrums every 1–2 weeks; before process fixes, "A developer would approach me... 'We need to deploy something to production today.' We'd have to drop what we were doing"; deployments took 6–8 hours, later 2–4. — [medium.com/@antelm1978](https://medium.com/@antelm1978/my-journey-as-a-release-manager-b2a613f065b1)
- "The go/no-go used to be a meeting because the evidence was scattered. Someone opened six tabs, pinged whoever ran the acceptance pass, and tried to remember whether the migration had actually been rehearsed." — [getautonoma.com](https://getautonoma.com/blog/release-management-process)
- Spreadsheet runbooks: "all the burden is on the person running the event to communicate with everyone involved to let them know when to start their task, find out status updates, and inform stakeholders"; "if things fall behind, the Excel spreadsheet has to be manually updated which is laborious and time consuming"; coordination happens "through emails, phone calls and messaging apps". — [cutover.com blog](https://cutover.com/blog/how-create-runbook-tools-techniques-efficiency)
- Bank paperwork overhead: at Morgan Stanley, "One engineer found 3 different Jiras, a change ticket, and 81 individual steps just to get paperwork created and approved" for a single-line change. — [teamtopologies.com](https://teamtopologies.com/news-blogs-newsletters/when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking)
- CAB preparation: publish RFC template and deadline "48-72 hours prior", "return incomplete ones", "Score each RFC against a consistent risk matrix pre-meeting", notify owners of outcomes "within hours", review failed/rolled-back changes at next CAB. — [itdevtech.com](https://itdevtech.com/blog/it-change-advisory-board-how-to-run-cab-that-works)

## 2. Regulated-industry release governance

### 2.1 What auditors ask for on a release

**SOX ITGC (change management control family)** — five expectations, each with ITSM evidence and the auditor test — [changeriskintel.com](https://changeriskintel.com/posts/sox-change-control-requirements-mapped-to-itsm/):

| Control | Quote | ServiceNow evidence | Jira evidence | Auditor test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Segregation of duties | "The person who wrote or configured the code change cannot be the same person who moves it into production." | `Assignment group` + workflow state restrictions | Restricted transition permissions; requester excluded from approver group | Approver/requester independent of author |
| Documented, risk-based approval | "Every change needs an identifiable approver... captured before the change ships, not after." | `Approvals` related list (timestamped, named) | Approval field with named approvers per transition | Sample tickets; approval exists pre-deployment |
| Testing evidence | "Test results, UAT sign-off, or a QA log attached to the change record, proportional to the change's risk." | `Test plan` / `Test results` attachment | Linked Test issue or QA sign-off field | Pull attachment |
| Backout plan | "Auditors want to see that the organization planned for failure, not just success." | `Backout plan` required field | Custom backout field (workflow validator) | Plan is "specific, not generic" |
| Retention | "Retain full ticket history... for at least the audit period plus one year." | Ticket history + edit audit trail; export to GRC repository | Atlassian retention; export to SOX repository | Historical sample retrieval; "no purged records" |

Also: `Implementation plan` "(required before CAB approval)"; `Close code` + `Close notes` (success / with issues / unsuccessful). Audit-ready CAB checklist includes: unique ticket ID; risk/impact classification with justification; named approver independent of author, timestamped pre-deployment; implementation plan; test evidence; backout plan; "Deployment by someone without commit/authoring rights on the same change"; closure code/notes; PIR noted for "successful with issues"/"unsuccessful"; full history exportable. Common PCAOB-pattern deficiencies translated: "missing attachments, approvals logged *after* deployment, or 'standard change' used to bypass CAB review." Emergency changes: "both need documentation and retrospective approval, not a blank exception."

SafePaaS list of what auditors review: approval records and routing, test results, SoD analyses at entitlement level, "Consolidated audit logs and tamper-proof records", incident workflows/remediation. Common findings: "Incomplete audit trails from scattered or manual documentation", "Unauthorized changes due to weak approval workflows", "Missing or inadequate testing evidence", "Lack of centralized visibility across applications." — [safepaas.com](https://www.safepaas.com/itgc/key-change-management-controls-auditors-look-for-in-sox-itgc-audits/)

**SOC 2 CC8.1** — control text: "The entity authorizes, designs, develops or acquires, configures, documents, tests, approves, and implements changes to infrastructure, data, software, and procedures to meet its objectives." Evidence: change tickets "with descriptions, risk assessments, and approval sign-offs"; Git records showing branch protection and PR merge history; "Change Log documenting all production changes with dates, requestors, and approvers"; "Deployment confirmation linking changes to approved requests"; system audit logs with timestamps. — [fex.truvocyber.com CC8.1](https://fex.truvocyber.com/browse/soc2/CC8.1). Sampling: auditors sample "20–40 production deployments from across the observation period"; "a single self-merge (a developer merging their own PR without a reviewer) in a sample of 30 will generate an exception"; "The person who writes the change should not be the person who approves it"; emergency changes need "post-change review within 24–48 hours confirming the change was appropriate." — [auditpath.io](https://www.auditpath.io/blog/soc2-change-management)

**PCI DSS Requirement 6 change control** (v3.2.1 numbering 6.4.5.x; carried into v4.0 as 6.5.1): impact documented "so that all affected parties can properly plan"; "Changes require documented change approval by authorized parties"; "Perform functionality test to verify that the change does not adversely affect the security of the system"; "Establish back-out procedures for changes". Environment controls: "Separate development and test environments from live environments" (6.4.1); "Separation of duties between development, testing and live environments" (6.4.2); no live PANs in test (6.4.3); test data/accounts removed before go-live (6.4.4). — [pcidssguide.com/pci-dss-requirement-6](https://pcidssguide.com/pci-dss-requirement-6/)

**FFIEC (bank examiners, Fiserv-relevant):** Under the AIO booklet's "Managing Change" action summary, examiners review "Policies, standards, and procedures for managing changes to the AIO environment", "Segregation of duties between those requesting, approving, testing, and implementing changes", "Processes for emergency or expedited changes", and "Post-implementation reviews to validate that changes achieved intended results". — [FFIEC AIO booklet (FLOFR mirror PDF)](https://flofr.gov/docs/default-source/documents/federal-financial-institutions-examination-council-information-technology-examination-booklet.pdf?sfvrsn=be80fc43_3). The 2024 Development, Acquisition & Maintenance booklet covers "Maintenance and change control processes" and "does not impose new requirements on examined entities". — [fdic.gov FIL 2024](https://fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2024/updated-ffiec-it-examination-handbook-development). Secondary summary of DA&M: routine vs major vs emergency changes; emergency changes "involving expedited processes with immediate risk assessments and rollback plans"; documentation must maintain "detailed records of all changes, including purpose, scope, and outcomes". — [netbankaudit.com](https://www.netbankaudit.com/resources/ffiec-development-acquisition-and-maintenance-booklet)

**UK FCA finding cited in a banking DORA talk:** FCA analyzed >1 million production changes; CABs approved >90% of submissions, "some firms rejected zero changes in 2019"; risk reducers found were frequent releases, modern architecture, automated infra provisioning, agile methods. — [teamtopologies.com](https://teamtopologies.com/news-blogs-newsletters/when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking) (secondary; I could not open the FCA source directly)

### 2.3 ServiceNow-style CHG record fields (what's mandatory / expected)

Field dictionary definitions from ServiceNow's own field help (Planning tab) — [ServiceNow Community thread](https://www.servicenow.com/community/itsm-forum/change-management-fields-definition-document/m-p/2665583):
- **Justification**: "Enter benefits of implementing this change and impact if this change is not implemented"
- **Implementation Plan**: "Enter sequential steps to implement this change. In addition, enter dependencies between steps and assignee details for each step"
- **Risk and Impact Analysis**: "Enter potential risks and impact associated with this change. Include impact details if this change implementation is unsuccessful"
- **Backout Plan**: "Enter steps to revert the change to its state prior to implementation. Include information regarding when the change can be backed out during implementation and if the change window includes time to backout"
- **Test Plan**: "Enter details of planned and completed tests prior to implementation that indicate the potential success of this change. Enter details of planned post-implementation tests to confirm success of this change"

Full form as deployed at a large university (representative of a standard OOTB build) — [UT Austin ServiceNow KB](https://ut.service-now.com/kb_view.do?sys_kb_id=0cf724061b47b994c9eeeb93604bcb47&sysparm_class_name=kb_knowledge):
- *Ticket*: Number (auto), Requester, Type of Change, State, Risk ("auto-generates based on assessment answers"), Assignment Group, Configuration Item
- *Planning*: Justification, Implementation Plan, Risk and Impact Analysis, Back-out Plan, Test Plan, **Communication Plan** ("Outline of customer notifications"), Notify Dependent CI Owners, Maintenance Type (Disruptive/Non-Disruptive)
- *Schedule*: Requested By Date/Time, Planned Start, Planned End, Actual Start, Actual End, CAB Delegate
- *Risk Assessment*: Security Sensitive, End Users Impacted, ITIL Users Impacted, Risk Assessment Value (RAV), Outage Scope and Complexity, Location and Customers Impacted, Business Impact, Back-out Rollback Plan, Experience With Change, **CAB Required** (checkbox)
- *Notes*: Watch List, Work Notes List, Additional Comments (customer-visible), Work Notes
- States: Create → Assess → Authorize → Schedule → Implement → Review → Close (Standard/Expedited/Emergency skip Assess)

Additional fields from a second deployment — [Berkeley ServiceNow KB](https://berkeley.service-now.com/kb_view.do?sysparm_article=KB0011601): Category, Priority, Impact (low/medium/high), **Outage** (Planned/None/Unplanned), Assigned To, Short Description ("verb-noun-outcome format"), CAB recommendation, **CAB date**; **Close code: Successful / Successful with issues / Unsuccessful**, Close notes. Approval path: New → Request Approval (Assess) → Assignment Group approval (Authorize) → CAB approval (Scheduled) → Implement → Review/Close.

Change types and paths (MSP documentation) — [netenrich.com](https://support.netenrich.com/hc/en-us/articles/7235124364573-Change-Requests-in-ServiceNow): Standard = "Pre-authorized, low-risk Changes that follow a well-known procedure"; Normal = New → Assess (technical approvals) → Authorize (CAB for high/moderate risk) → Scheduled → Implement → Review → Closed; Emergency = skips Assess, "ECAB group" approval only.

Power BI-facing field names from `change_request`: `sys_id, type, state, planned_start_date, end_date, close_code, cab_date, u_rollback_performed, assignment_group`; `type` = emergency/normal/standard; `state` = "draft, assess, authorize, scheduled, implement, review, closed". — [lets-viz.com](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/servicenow-change-management-dashboard-cab-power-bi-guide)

**Lead-time rules** (ServiceNow community, practitioner): lead time = "how much time (in terms of days) are in advance before a Change can be implemented", keyed on "Planned Start Date, Priority and Change Type"; a commenter's risk-based minimums for normal changes: Very High 21 days, High 14, Moderate 7, Low/No risk 2; enforcement options: warning message, auto-retype, or extra approvals for short notice. — [ServiceNow Community: Change Management Lead Times](https://www.servicenow.com/community/itsm-articles/change-management-lead-times/ta-p/2299624)

**ITIL 4 framing:** change types standard/normal/emergency; "not all changes require CAB review"; route "approval authority according to risk rather than routing every change through a central board"; Change Authority can be a delegated team, peer review, or "Automated approval processes in CI/CD pipelines"; "Change enablement is designed to facilitate and accelerate change safely, not slow it down." — [itsm.tools](https://itsm.tools/change-enablement/)

**PIR trigger rules** (example policy): PIR mandatory when a change "created an unknown/unexpected impact", "failed", "was not properly communicated", or had "further impact than indicated from the review process"; CAB reviews the PIR. — [csueastbay.edu PIR](https://www.csueastbay.edu/its/processes-policies/change-management/pir.html)

**Freeze / blackout**: implemented as ServiceNow "change blackout windows"; collisions can "send an additional approval to the Freeze manager"; "Change Chill" alternative allows "minor enhancements and basic platform support changes"; "most typical change freeze or chill period is over the Christmas holidays". — [ServiceNow Community freeze thread](https://www.servicenow.com/community/itsm-forum/is-a-change-freeze-necessary-in-servicenow/m-p/511369). Freeze policy definition: "a time- and scope-limited control that prevents or restricts changes to production systems to reduce risk during high-impact periods, while providing controlled exception paths and telemetry"; exceptions need multi-approver workflow, justification, "TTL (time-to-live) on temporary exceptions", centralized audit logging; typical windows: revenue events, "Quarterly financial reporting, audit windows", multi-region cutovers, holiday staffing, post-incident. — [sreschool.com](https://sreschool.com/blog/freeze-policy/)

## 3. Release metrics that matter

### 3.1 DORA keys — definitions, computation, thresholds

Official definitions and computation from Google's Four Keys project — [cloud.google.com blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/devops-sre/using-the-four-keys-to-measure-your-devops-performance):
- **Deployment Frequency**: "How often an organization successfully releases to production." Computed from successful deployments only; "falls into the Daily bucket when the median number of days per week with at least one successful deployment is equal to or greater than three."
- **Lead Time for Changes**: "The amount of time it takes a commit to get into production." "For every deployment, you need to maintain a list of all the changes included in the deployment... using triggers with a SHA mapping back to the commits"; join changes→deployments by SHA, take the **median**.
- **Change Failure Rate**: "The percentage of deployments causing a failure in production." "how many deployments were attempted, and how many resulted in failures in production?" — link incidents to deployments by deployment ID.
- **Time to Restore Service**: "How long it takes an organization to recover from a failure in production." Needs incident created time, resolved time, and "when a deployment resolved said incident."

Evolution (2023: MTTR renamed "failed deployment recovery time", measuring recovery "after a change to production caused an impairment"; 2024: fifth metric "deployment rework rate" = "percentage of deployments that are unplanned work to fix bugs"; current grouping: Throughput = change lead time, deployment frequency, failed deployment recovery time; Instability = change fail rate, deployment rework rate). — [dora.dev/insights/dora-metrics-history](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-metrics-history/), [cd.foundation](https://cd.foundation/blog/2025/10/16/dora-5-metrics/) ("Rework rate: The number of unplanned deployments divided by the total number of deployments"; CFR: "The percentage of changes resulting in a fault, incident, or rollback").

Simple formulas as stated by a vendor guide — [octopus.com](https://octopus.com/devops/metrics/dora-metrics/): DF = "Number of Deployments ÷ Number of Days"; Lead time = "Sum of all lead times ÷ number of deployments"; CFR = "(Failed Deployments ÷ Total Deployments) × 100"; Recovery = "Total Recovery Time ÷ Number of Failed Deployments". (Note Google's reference implementation uses medians, not means.)

2024 performance levels — [infrazen.io](https://infrazen.io/dora-metrics) (cross-checked with [octopus.com](https://octopus.com/devops/metrics/dora-metrics/)):

| Metric | Elite | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Change lead time | < 1 day | 1 day – 1 week | 1 week – 1 month | 1 – 6 months |
| Deployment frequency | On demand (multiple/day) | daily – weekly | weekly – monthly | monthly – 6-monthly |
| Change fail rate | 5% | 20% | 10% | 40% |
| Failed deployment recovery time | < 1 hour | < 1 day | < 1 day | 1 week – 1 month |

(2024 respondent split: Elite 19%, High 22%, Medium 35%, Low 25%. Note the 2024 anomaly where High CFR > Medium CFR is real in the report data.)

Change-approval findings you'll want to cite in the scoping doc: teams with external approval (CAB/senior manager) were "2.6 times more likely to be low performers"; teams with a clear, understood change process were "1.8 times more likely to be elite performers"; "no evidence was found to support the hypothesis that a more formal, external review process was associated with lower change fail rates." DORA's suggested measures: "Percentage of changes requiring manual approval before production deployment", "Time spent waiting for approvals from external bodies", "Proportion of peer-reviewed vs. externally-approved changes." — [DORA 2019 report PDF](https://dora.dev/research/2019/dora-report/2019-dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-report.pdf), [dora.dev/capabilities/streamlining-change-approval](https://dora.dev/capabilities/streamlining-change-approval/)

| **Release on-time rate** | "% of planned releases executed within agreed window"; target "85–95% on-time for standard releases" — [devopsschool](https://www.devopsschool.com/blog/release-manager-role-blueprint-responsibilities-skills-kpis-and-career-path/); Enov8 lists "Project Releases Delivered on Time" and "Project Release Delay" — [enov8](https://www.enov8.com/blog/holistic-release-management-metrics/) | *(derived)* count(releases where actual_go_live ≤ baselined_planned_go_live + tolerance) / count(releases completed in period). Requires a frozen baseline date distinct from the current planned date. |
| **Release cycle time** | "Release Cycle Time: End Date – Start Date" — [apwide](https://www.apwide.com/release-management-kpis/) | Per release: actual_end − actual_start (or created→closed). |
| **Gate cycle time** | No direct source names "gate cycle time"; nearest: "CAB cycle time: RFC submission to authorization decision" — [itdevtech](https://itdevtech.com/blog/it-change-advisory-board-how-to-run-cab-that-works); "Approval cycle time: Time from change submission to approval" — [devopsschool](https://www.devopsschool.com/blog/release-manager-role-blueprint-responsibilities-skills-kpis-and-career-path/); "Lead time distribution by approval stage (measured in hours)" from `sys_audit` state transitions — [lets-viz](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/servicenow-change-management-dashboard-cab-power-bi-guide) | *(derived)* For each gate: exited_at − entered_at, from the gate-state transition log; report median and p90 per gate. ServiceNow practitioners pull the `change_request_metric` table filtered to Definition "Change Approval", Value "Authorize", using the `start` column, and must dedupe because "it picks up EVERY entry in the logs" when a change cycles states — [ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/platform-analytics-forum/report-on-change-request-metric-table-against-change-approval/m-p/1227493) |
| **CAB approval lead time** | Same as above; ServiceNow lead-time rules key on planned start vs. now — [ServiceNow lead times](https://www.servicenow.com/community/itsm-articles/change-management-lead-times/ta-p/2299624) | *(derived)* approval_timestamp − submitted_for_approval_timestamp; separately, planned_start − approval_timestamp to test policy minimums (e.g., 21/14/7/2 days by risk). |
| **Scope change / churn** | "Release scope stability: % of scope changes after freeze", target "<10% scope change after freeze" — [devopsschool](https://www.devopsschool.com/blog/release-manager-role-blueprint-responsibilities-skills-pis-and-career-path/); Enov8: "Number of Project De-Scoped", "Number of Systems De-Scoped", "Number of Features De-Scoped" — [enov8](https://www.enov8.com/blog/holistic-release-management-metrics/) | *(derived)* (items added + items removed after scope-freeze timestamp) / items in scope at freeze. Requires an immutable scope snapshot at the freeze gate. |
| **Defect escape rate** | "(Defects Found Post-Release / Total Number of Defects) × 100"; "a good Defect Escape Rate is typically below 5%" — [kpidepot](https://kpidepot.com/kpi/defect-escape-rate); Apwide "Defect Rate: (Defects Found After Release / Total Releases)" — [apwide](https://www.apwide.com/release-management-kpis/) | Defects linked to the release with found_in = production / all defects linked to the release. |
| **Rollback rate** | "Tracks the percentage of releases that require rollback due to issues" — [aviator](https://www.aviator.co/blog/kpis-for-release-management/); target "Low single digits in mature orgs" — [devopsschool]; freeze-policy target "<1%" — [sreschool](https://sreschool.com/blog/freeze-policy/); Power BI: "Rollback rate percentage (closed changes reversed)" using `u_rollback_performed` — [lets-viz](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/servicenow-change-management-dashboard-cab-power-bi-guide) | count(releases with rollback_performed = true) / count(releases deployed). |
| **Change success rate** | "percentage without incident or unplanned rollback" — [itdevtech](https://itdevtech.com/blog/it-change-advisory-board-how-to-run-cab-that-works) | count(close_code = Successful) / count(closed changes); "Successful with issues" tracked separately. |
| **Emergency change ratio** | "rising proportion signals process bypass" — [itdevtech] | count(type = emergency) / count(all changes). |
| **Blocker aging** | No industry-standard formula found; the one glossary entry is generic — [weaveos](https://weaveos.com/glossary/blocker-aging). Related: "Open Defect Exposure: unresolved defects mapped to the affected journey, severity, and customer impact" and "Evidence Freshness: time since the relevant staging run completed successfully" — [dev.to quality metrics](https://dev.to/k4224/software-quality-metrics-a-practice-guide-for-release-decisions-kh0) | *(derived)* For each open blocker: now − raised_at; report count by age bucket and max age per release; alert when age exceeds gate SLA. |
| **Runbook step variance (actual vs planned)** | Cutover's PIR dashboard: "Performance vs Planned" graph shows "planned duration of all tasks against the actual time it took for those tasks to be completed, grouped by stream and teams"; "Lateness" component gives "a breakdown of all the tasks on the runbook by how late they are based on the planned schedule" with configurable thresholds; critical-path burndown; green = earlier/faster, orange = later/slower — [help.cutover.com PIR dashboard](https://help.cutover.com/en/articles/4585366-post-implementation-review-pir-dashboard). CutoverCenter: "Overwriting planned values with actual values destroys the comparison needed for forecasting and learning." — [cutovercenter.com](https://www.cutovercenter.com/resources/what-is-a-cutover-plan) | Per step: actual_duration − planned_duration; actual_start − planned_start (lateness); aggregate by stream/team; total runbook variance. |
| **Freeze violations** | Freeze KPIs: "Deployment blocks (aiming for 100% during windows)", "Emergency deploys ≤2 per month", "Exception approval time <15 minutes median", "Audit completeness 100%" — [sreschool](https://sreschool.com/blog/freeze-policy/) | count(deployments with actual_start inside a freeze window AND no approved exception) per window; plus exception count and exception approval time. |
| **Post-release validation time** | "Time from deployment to 'release confirmed'" — [devopsschool] | release_confirmed_at − deployment_complete_at. |
| **Change record completeness** | "% of changes with required fields/evidence", target "95–100%" — [devopsschool] | count(changes with all mandatory fields + attachments present) / count(changes). |
| **Communication timeliness** | "Notifications sent within agreed SLA", "100% for major releases" — [devopsschool] | count(scheduled comms sent by T-minus deadline) / count(scheduled comms). |
| **ART predictability** | planned vs achieved business value, "roughly 80 to 100 percent" — [agility-at-scale](https://agility-at-scale.com/safe/pi-planning/) | Σ achieved BV / Σ planned BV per team per PI. |
| **Change-related incident rate** | "incidents caused by changes as percentage of total" — [itdevtech] | count(incidents with caused_by_change link) / count(incidents). |
| **Enterprise release size** | "Size of Enterprise Release by Projects / System Deployments / Features"; "Root Cause of Project Release Delay" — [enov8](https://www.enov8.com/blog/holistic-release-management-metrics/) | Counts per master release; delay root-cause as a coded field. |

## 4. Post-release scorecard — what practitioners include

- **Release completion report**: "what shipped, validation outcome, known issues" — [devopsschool](https://www.devopsschool.com/blog/release-manager-role-blueprint-responsibilities-skills-kpis-and-career-path/). Also tracked per release: "Day-of-release surprises" ("Critical issues discovered during cutover", target "Near zero"), "PIR action completion rate" (">80–90% on-time").
- **Change closure**: Close code (Successful / Successful with issues / Unsuccessful) + Close notes; PIR mandatory for "with issues" / "unsuccessful". — [changeriskintel](https://changeriskintel.com/posts/sox-change-control-requirements-mapped-to-itsm/), [Berkeley KB](https://berkeley.service-now.com/kb_view.do?sysparm_article=KB0011601)
- **Cutover PIR dashboard** (tool-native scorecard): runbook actual start/end/duration vs planned; Performance vs Planned by stream/team; Lateness distribution; critical-path burndown; User Participation (total users, viewed, "performed an action in the runbook"). — [help.cutover.com](https://help.cutover.com/en/articles/4585366-post-implementation-review-pir-dashboard)
- **Post-release checklist items**: immediate monitoring of error rates, validation testing, user impact assessment, documentation cleanup, "Schedule team retrospective meeting", "document lessons learned". — [launchdarkly.com](https://launchdarkly.com/blog/release-management-checklist/)
- **Retrospective structure** (GitLab's monthly release-post retro): items tagged "Something that went well", "Something that didn't go well", "Something we can improve", "Something for discussion"; release post manager tracks feedback from Slack/MRs and closes threads with ✅; "Items affecting the next release workflow must close by month's end." — [gitlab.com issue 35236](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-com/www-gitlab-com/-/issues/35236)
- **Release status/summary report template sections**: project details (release number, stakeholders), release overview (objectives, scope, major changes), schedule and milestones, issues and risks ("severity, impact, and proposed mitigation"), resource allocation, quality assurance ("testing activities, bug tracking, and validation procedures"), feedback and lessons learned, visuals. — [itgov-docs.com](https://www.itgov-docs.com/blogs/release-management/release-status-report-template)
- **Release-decision metrics that carry into the scorecard**: "Critical Journey Status (pass, fail, blocked, or unknown)", "New Failure Count" vs last known-good build, "Open Defect Exposure", "Evidence Freshness", "Test Validity" (percentage of results run against the intended commit/environment); flow metrics "Time from failure to owner assignment", "Time from assignment to root-cause classification". — [dev.to](https://dev.to/k4224/software-quality-metrics-a-practice-guide-for-release-decisions-kh0)
- **Go/no-go decision record retained as evidence**: timestamps, gate states, evidence links, decision owners, yellow-state exceptions with expiry. — [oneuptime.com](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-08-06-launch-day-go-no-go/view)
- Octopus's 7-step model ends with "Post-deployment and Feedback": performance monitoring data, user feedback, issue logs; retrospective documents "what went well, what could have been done better, and... lessons learned". — [octopus.com](https://octopus.com/devops/software-deployments/release-management/)

Synthesised scorecard (union of the above, for the scoping doc): release ID/version, planned vs actual go-live and window, on-time flag, scope at freeze vs shipped (added/removed), gate timeline with per-gate cycle times, CAB/approval lead time, close code, incidents/rollback/hotfix flags with links, defects escaped, runbook variance summary (total, worst stream, lateness histogram), comms sent vs planned, evidence completeness %, hypercare exit status and known-issues log, retro actions with owners/due dates, PIR required (Y/N) and status.

## 5. Common failure modes (practitioner accounts)

**Gate theater / rubber-stamp CAB**
- "Weekly meetings where rubber stamps are handed out like Halloween candy"; forms filled with "boilerplate risk assessments nobody reads"; approval chains so long that by the time approval arrives teams have "pivoted, been reorg'd, or burned out"; "Auditors only verify controls exist, not whether they prevent actual problems"; "maturity isn't about how many signoffs you collect. It's about how quickly you can adapt without losing control." *(practitioner opinion)* — [willkelly.substack.com](https://willkelly.substack.com/p/change-management-is-broken-and-its)
- ITIL 4 lead architect *(practitioner opinion)*: CAB's "A" was reinterpreted from Advisory to Approval; result is "late-in-the-game mechanism[s]", "Misaligned accountability: Teams remain accountable for outcomes while boards avoid responsibility", "Risk aversion masquerading as quality control". — [kaimarkaru.medium.com](https://kaimarkaru.medium.com/cab-in-the-age-of-devops-dd6f03b12af8)
- Accelerate data: "external approvals were negatively correlated with lead time, deployment frequency, and restore time, and had no correlation with change fail rate"; CABs cause "larger, riskier deployments" because batching. — [octopus.com/blog](https://octopus.com/blog/change-advisory-boards-dont-work)
- FCA: >90% of >1M changes approved; some firms "rejected zero changes in 2019". — [teamtopologies.com](https://teamtopologies.com/news-blogs-newsletters/when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking)
- CAB dysfunction table: rubber-stamp ("Change manager shouldn't pre-approve everything"), too many changes per meeting ("Attention fatigue on high-risk items"), no-shows, late submissions, "No CMDB connection: Impact assessments lack accuracy". — [itdevtech.com](https://itdevtech.com/blog/it-change-advisory-board-how-to-run-cab-that-works)
- Audit-side counterparts: "approvals logged *after* deployment", "'standard change' used to bypass CAB review", generic backout plans. — [changeriskintel.com](https://changeriskintel.com/posts/sox-change-control-requirements-mapped-to-itsm/)

**Stale spreadsheets and email-driven status**
- Spreadsheet runbooks: manual updates when behind, "version control and governance" problems, coordination "through emails, phone calls and messaging apps". — [cutover.com](https://cutover.com/blog/how-create-runbook-tools-techniques-efficiency)
- Service NSW "relied on spreadsheets and emails to manage their environments", had "No single source of truth for release information, deployment plans, or test environment status". — [planview.com case study](https://www.planview.com/resources/case-study/service-nsw/)
- Evidence scattered: "Someone opened six tabs, pinged whoever ran the acceptance pass, and tried to remember whether the migration had actually been rehearsed." — [getautonoma.com](https://getautonoma.com/blog/release-management-process)
- "Information scattered across multiple tools... Risks hidden until final meeting." — [qualitybridgeconsulting](https://qualitybridgeconsulting.substack.com/p/the-gono-go-meeting-should-be-a-confirmation)
- "Slow Stakeholder Response: Delays in stakeholder feedback block releases and frustrate developers." — [clickup.com](https://clickup.com/blog/release-management-process/)

**Heroics during cutover / manual prep**
- 6–8 hours to prep a release plan + scripts; hand-edited batch files; ad-hoc same-day production requests; incidents "happening almost daily". — [medium.com/@antelm1978](https://medium.com/@antelm1978/my-journey-as-a-release-manager-b2a613f065b1)
- "A build can pass every functional gate above and still take the application down if the deploy script silently fails to apply a migration." — [getautonoma.com](https://getautonoma.com/blog/release-management-process)

**No rollback rehearsal**
- Failure pattern to avoid: "a command exists but has not been tested with the current schema and data." — [oneuptime.com](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-08-06-launch-day-go-no-go/view)
- Checklist: "Test rollback procedures for all changes"; kill switches configured before release. — [launchdarkly.com](https://launchdarkly.com/blog/release-management-checklist/)
- CAB's three questions per change: blast radius, "rollback plan credibility", non-prod testing completion. — [itdevtech.com](https://itdevtech.com/blog/it-change-advisory-board-how-to-run-cab-that-works)
- Cutover: "Carry out dry runs and walkthroughs with application and infrastructure resources." — [cutover.com](https://cutover.com/blog/how-create-runbook-tools-techniques-efficiency)

**Integration / data-sync failures (Jira ↔ ServiceNow, exports)**
- Jira export pitfalls: "Field mismatches are the most frequent complaint when teams move Jira data into another system" (column order, delimiters, date formats); custom fields "require explicit mapping"; large exports "can exceed server timeouts or memory limits"; multi-value fields "semicolon-delimited"; dates vary by instance settings so use ISO 8601. — [ngpilot.com](https://ngpilot.com/blog/faq-exporting-jira-issues)
- ServiceNow metric tables record every state transition, so approval-time reports double-count unless deduped. — [ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/platform-analytics-forum/report-on-change-request-metric-table-against-change-approval/m-p/1227493)
- Power BI over the ServiceNow Table API: "Use scheduled refresh (nightly) rather than DirectQuery in production - the Table API is not optimised for live query volumes." — [lets-viz.com](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/servicenow-change-management-dashboard-cab-power-bi-guide)
- (The Zigiwave "fallout of Jira–ServiceNow sync" article could not be fetched — robots redirect loop.)

**Overwriting the baseline**
- "Overwriting planned values with actual values destroys the comparison needed for forecasting and learning." — [cutovercenter.com](https://www.cutovercenter.com/resources/what-is-a-cutover-plan)

**Freeze misuse**
- Blanket freezes described as "an antiquated idea"; exceptions without TTL become permanent; single-approver exception paths bottleneck. — [ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/itsm-forum/is-a-change-freeze-necessary-in-servicenow/m-p/511369), [sreschool.com](https://sreschool.com/blog/freeze-policy/)

**Post-release abandonment**
- "Teams will be at risk of inviting more work if closing deployment is not practiced post-release." — [clickup.com](https://clickup.com/blog/release-management-process/)

*(No source found for "lost drafts" specifically; the closest is the Cutover CSV re-import rule that "overwrite all existing tasks" replaces current content, and the ServiceNow note that changes cycling through states leave multiple log entries.)*

## 6. Common input/output formats in practice

### Imports
- **Excel/CSV runbooks** — Cutover's task-upload template columns: `Task ID` (numeric, "#" prefix), `Title` (mandatory), `Predecessor IDs`, `Description`, `Stream`, `Sub Stream`, `Task Type`, `Planned Duration` ("dd:hh:mm"), `Task Level` (Level_1..3), `Assigned Team (Name)` ("You cannot assign individual users via CSV upload, only teams"), `Planned Fixed Date` (yyyy-mm-dd or dd/mm/yyyy), `Planned Fixed Time` (24h + timezone code), `Planned due date/time`, custom fields (must pre-exist). — [help.cutover.com task upload](https://help.cutover.com/en/articles/6171869-task-upload-template-guide). Limits: max 100 columns; "Linked tasks, including task dependencies or RTO-linked tasks, are not supported in CSV uploads"; import allowed only in Draft/Planning (or Paused/Live for non-dynamic) states; append vs "overwrite all existing tasks". — [help.cutover.com export/import](https://help.cutover.com/en/articles/3850069-export-and-import-tasks)
- **Cutover-plan activity fields** (vendor-neutral): "Unique reference and unambiguous activity name", "Workstream, phase and accountable owner", "Planned start, finish and duration", "Predecessors and successor implications", "Completion criteria, evidence and checklist", "Executor, validator or approver where required", "Contingency information and decision deadline"; milestones "legacy freeze, migration complete, business validation complete, go/no-go and production release". — [cutovercenter.com](https://www.cutovercenter.com/resources/what-is-a-cutover-plan)
- **Jira CSV**: only `Summary` is mandatory; date formats must follow Java `SimpleDateFormat` set in the wizard; multi-value fields (Fix Version, Affects Version, Components, Labels) by repeating the column header; subtasks via `Issue ID` / `Parent ID` columns; cannot create users, projects, custom fields, or resolutions during import. — [confluence.atlassian.com](https://confluence.atlassian.com/adminjiraserver/importing-data-from-csv-938847533.html)
- **CMDB CI lists**: CI is a core CHG field; CAB advice: "RFC must reference affected configuration items" and "Notify Dependent CI Owners" is a form field. — [itdevtech](https://itdevtech.com/blog/it-change-advisory-board-how-to-run-cab-that-works), [UT Austin KB](https://ut.service-now.com/kb_view.do?sys_kb_id=0cf724061b47b994c9eeeb93604bcb47&sysparm_class_name=kb_knowledge)
- **ServiceNow Table API** (`/api/now/table/change_request?sysparm_fields=...`) with a service account holding `itil` or `change_manager` role; `sys_audit` for state timestamps. — [lets-viz.com](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/servicenow-change-management-dashboard-cab-power-bi-guide)

### Exports
- **Audit/evidence packs**: SOX guidance says "export to GRC repository" / "export to SOX repository" with "Full ticket history retained and exportable for audit period". — [changeriskintel](https://changeriskintel.com/posts/sox-change-control-requirements-mapped-to-itsm/). SOC 2 auditors collect "Repository configuration screenshots", "Production deployment samples with corresponding pull request records, reviewer approvals, and CI pass status", emergency change records with post-change review. — [auditpath.io](https://www.auditpath.io/blog/soc2-change-management). GitLab's release-evidence "chain of custody" export fields: Commit SHA, Author, MR ID, Merged By, Source/Target Branch, Group, Project, Approver(s), Milestone(s), Release name, Pipeline ID, Released At; "Compliance-minded organizations rely on evidence artifacts to pass their internal or external audits." — [gitlab.com issue 213365](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab/-/issues/213365)
- **Runbook exports**: CSV or XLSX with timezone selection; includes standard fields, custom fields with IDs, and an "OUTPUT FIELDS" section that must be stripped before re-import. — [help.cutover.com](https://help.cutover.com/en/articles/3850069-export-and-import-tasks)
- **CSV/API to Power BI**: nightly scheduled refresh from ServiceNow Table API; KPIs: lead time by approval stage, rollback rate, volume by type, success rate by team. — [lets-viz.com](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/servicenow-change-management-dashboard-cab-power-bi-guide)
- **Calendar ICS feeds**: long-running Jira request for iCal feeds of versions/release due dates (JRACLOUD-2971, opened 2004, 34 votes, closed 2023) — [jira.atlassian.com](https://jira.atlassian.com/browse/JRACLOUD-2971); users want "Release time periods (start date to release date), not just publication dates", overlapping-release visualization, and "iCAL export for external calendar tool integration"; the most-requested feature across responses was iCAL export. — [community.atlassian.com](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-questions/Show-releases-in-a-calendar-view/qaq-p/706030)
- **Status emails / notification templates**: T-minus email templates (see §7); ServiceNow "Communication Plan" field = "Outline of customer notifications". — [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/large-migration-governance-playbook/task-follow-communication-gates.html), [UT Austin KB](https://ut.service-now.com/kb_view.do?sys_kb_id=0cf724061b47b994c9eeeb93604bcb47&sysparm_class_name=kb_knowledge)
- **Release notes / user documentation** as formal release deliverables (government EPLC checklist): "Have release notes been created describing what's new?", "Has it been verified that any documentation distributed with the current release indeed matches that release?", "Has the new system release been formally announced?", "Have the appropriate regulatory offices been contacted?" — [hhs.gov EPLC](https://www.hhs.gov/ocio/eplc/EPLC%20Archive%20Documents/29-Release%20Strategy/eplc_release_strategy_checklist.pdf)

## 7. Communication practice

### Audiences
- Internal packages by team: Sales (value prop, pricing impact), Customer Service ("support resources, anticipated customer questions"), Product, Senior Leadership ("incredibly succinct email focused on business impact... literally keep it to 2-3 sentences") plus "simple 2-3min Loom"; public release notes structured as "Big features" (1–2), "Enhancements" (bullets), "Bug fixes"; marketing email limited to "2-5" highlights. *(practitioner opinion)* — [alexdebecker.substack.com](https://alexdebecker.substack.com/p/how-i-organise-my-product-releases)
- Hypercare war-room plan "states which channel is used for operations, which for executives, and which for end-user notices"; pre-prepared templates for "incident acknowledgment, stakeholder summaries, and user advisories"; a **known issues log** capturing workarounds, root-cause status, and whether issues block BAU handover; exit "when the platform is supportable by the target operating model, not when the project team is eager to move on." — [kogifi.com](https://www.kogifi.com/articles/what-is-hypercare)
- Go-live announcement recipients: team, stakeholders, staff and management, sponsor, peripheral executives, "Potentially the CEO". — [tacticalprojectmanager.com](https://www.tacticalprojectmanager.com/golive-email/)
- Ops/support: Creighton requires IT alerts "7 days prior to the effective date of the change"; approval criteria include "IT alert distribution". — [Creighton CAB](https://myservices.creighton.edu/TDClient/32/Portal/KB/Article/1065/Change-Advisory-Board-CAB-Operating-Procedures)

### Cadence — T-minus schedule (AWS migration governance playbook, the most concrete public T-minus model)
[docs.aws.amazon.com](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/large-migration-governance-playbook/task-follow-communication-gates.html)

| Gate | When | Activity / recipients |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | schedule created | Create repository; schedule T-28 |
| 2 | **T-28** | Commit meeting with application owners: wave overview, action items, confirm owners "need to provide test plans", "Review the escalation plan"; then send *T-28 communication email* |
| 3 | **T-21** | *T-21 communication email* to "application owners and business unit representatives" |
| 4 | **T-14** | Checkpoint meeting: review apps/servers, remaining tasks, confirm availability for cutover, "Confirm that test plans are ready"; send *T-14 email* |
| 5 | **T-7** | *T-7 email*; confirm attendance for T-1 and T-0 |
| 6 | **T-1** | Go/no-go "24-48 hours before cutover" with "all team members on the RACI matrix"; on go send *T-1 email*; on no-go "send an email to all stakeholders informing them of the decision" |
| 7 | **T-0** | *T-0 email* before cutover; cutover meeting; *cutover complete email* |
| 8 | T-0 | Hypercare begins |
| 9 | T+1–4 days | *Hypercare complete email* with "instructions for how to contact the cloud operations team"; notify infra team to decommission source |

Standard email templates are created once per gate ("T-28, T-21, T-14, T-7, T-1, T-0, Cutover complete, Hypercare complete") and customized "for the wave information and recipients."

Other cadence anchors: Fedora go/no-go notice 3 days prior, readiness meeting announced 5 days prior — [Fedora Go/No-Go](https://fedoraproject.org/wiki/Go_No_Go_Meeting), [Release Readiness](https://fedoraproject.org/wiki/Release_Readiness_Meetings); CAB RFC deadline "48-72 hours prior", outcomes "within hours" — [itdevtech](https://itdevtech.com/blog/it-change-advisory-board-how-to-run-cab-that-works); "Communication timeliness: Notifications sent within agreed SLA, 100% for major releases" — [devopsschool](https://www.devopsschool.com/blog/release-manager-role-blueprint-responsibilities-skills-kpis-and-career-path/).

### Standard sections — go/no-go communication
Synthesised from the sources that describe the decision content:
- Decision (GO / NO-GO / GO-with-conditions) and who made it (Decision Authority vs Release Commander split) — [oneuptime](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-08-06-launch-day-go-no-go/view), [Fedora](https://fedoraproject.org/wiki/Go_No_Go_Meeting)
- Gate/criteria status with evidence links; blocker status ("no unaddressed accepted blocker bugs") — [Fedora], [oneuptime]
- Known issues and "explicit risk acceptance" — [qualitybridgeconsulting](https://qualitybridgeconsulting.substack.com/p/the-gono-go-meeting-should-be-a-confirmation)
- Conditions/exceptions with expiry ("approved subject to completing load testing by Friday"; "yellow-state exceptions with expiration dates") — [itdevtech], [oneuptime]
- Timeline/window, impact, contact point ("Communicate release timeline, impact, and contact point") — [hector-reyesaleman.medium.com](https://hector-reyesaleman.medium.com/what-does-a-release-manager-do-ff468df1a88b)
- Rollback readiness statement (rollback "tested with the current schema and data") — [oneuptime]
- On no-go: new date (Fedora: slip one week) and notification to all stakeholders — [Fedora], [AWS]

## Implications worth carrying into the scoping doc (brief)

1. **Evidence, not approvals, is the audit unit.** Every gate approval needs: named approver ≠ author, timestamp strictly before deployment, linked test evidence, specific backout plan, and an immutable history exportable for "audit period + 1 year" (SOX practice) — with a 7-year ceiling for SOX-adjacent records. The PDF evidence pack should reproduce exactly the ServiceNow field set above (Justification, Implementation Plan, Risk & Impact Analysis, Backout Plan, Test Plan, Planned/Actual Start/End, CI list, Close code/notes, Approvals list with timestamps, CAB date).
2. **Baseline snapshots are load-bearing metrics inputs**: on-time %, scope churn, and runbook variance all require a frozen baseline (planned date, scope at freeze, planned step durations) that is never overwritten by actuals.
3. **Gate-state transition log** is the source for gate cycle time, CAB lead time, and blocker aging — design it as an append-only event table from day one and dedupe on read (the ServiceNow double-count problem).
4. **T-minus comms are templated per gate**, not per release; the tool should generate them from the master train's dates and recipients (RACI), and record send timestamps for the "communication timeliness" KPI.
5. **CSV import scope** that practitioners actually use: runbook tasks (Cutover columns), Jira issues (Summary-only mandatory, repeated headers for multi-value), CI lists; exports: CSV/XLSX runbooks, ICS calendar feed (a persistent unmet Jira need), CSV for Power BI, PDF evidence pack.
